    How it’s worked out

    Mortality comes from the Social Security Administration’s life table, the chance of dying within a year at every age. It’s an average, so it knows nothing about medical progress or anyone’s health. The table comes in two columns, which is why it asks man or woman.

    You only get a year together if you’re both alive for it, so the two survival curves are multiplied, not compared. Each year ahead counts the days you’d be in the same place, weighted by the chance you both make it.

    Days aren’t equal, so the drawing is in hours. A kid at home counts as 6 hours a day until school at 5, 4 until 13, then 3. Two adults at home count as 4, and a visit counts as 6. Rough numbers, from the American Time Use Survey.

    It’s an average with a name on it, not a forecast. Nothing you type leaves this page. The idea is Tim Urban’s.
