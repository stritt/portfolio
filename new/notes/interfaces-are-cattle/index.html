← Interfaces are cattle, not pets… 10.20.26

I recently received an email from a popular analytics company letting me know that I could now save the UIs I generate instead of recreating them each time. This was pitched as a time-saving feature.

I never responded, for two reasons. One, email is the worst way to get ahold of me. And two, it falls into the same trap that much of modern generative UI tooling does: spend time learning this new paradigm now, and it'll save you time in the future.

You could argue I'm just focused on the shiny object in front of me, but I think it's more likely rooted in the fact that tomorrow is unpredictable. What I want tomorrow probably doesn't map 1:1 to what I want today.

And when interfaces take seconds to generate, there's a real cost to learning how to preserve them. I now need to understand where these interfaces are saved, how to find them again, and how to edit them when my needs inevitably change. At some point, starting from scratch just feels easier.

Today we treat dashboards and interfaces like pets. Something precious that we love, maintain, nurture, and care for.

But in the not-too-distant future, I see that changing. Dashboards will become something we consume and then discard. A point-in-time generation that fills exactly the gap we need it to, answers our most pressing questions, and then gets out of the way.

That doesn't mean the underlying data or context should be disposable. But the interface itself? Maybe that's just packaging.

The future of generative UI isn't finding better ways to save dashboards. It's making them so cheap to create that we don't need to.
