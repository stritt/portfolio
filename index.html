cloudflare’s all that

August 22, 2026

It was about 2 years ago that Brayden and I were sitting in our tiny 120 sqft office heads down doing a full rewrite of Outerbase’s infrastructure, porting it over to Durable Objects and Workers for Platforms. I remember being so frustrated with the dashboard, but absolutely amazed when it all finally came together.

How could something so powerful be so… bad? There was next to no consistency, the calls were slow, and the navigation was confusing, but the end product almost made wading through the muck worth it. We joked that if only they gave us access to their dashboard we could fix her.

Then we got into their startup program, gave some talks together, and ended up being acquired. Since then we’ve been working hard to make that joke a reality, and honestly it hasn’t been as bad as I was thinking, mostly because the problem was never the platform.

You know that classic 90s movie trope? The one where the “ugly” girl just needed to get a haircut and lose the glasses and all of a sudden she was beautiful? Yeah, that’s Cloudflare. The dashboard needed to get out of its own way and let the actual core primitives shine through.

We were able to make this happen by doing a number of things. First we introduced a design system, what you now may know as Kumo. It started on day 1 with Brayden putting up his first PR to add Tailwind to the dashboard. Surprisingly this was a controversial topic with some developers, and that led to the next key unlock for making the redesign happen.

Having agency and being relentless. Thankfully a lot of good people who had a lot of sway had our backs, whether it was us being immovable on design decisions or bringing the startup mentality into building frontend (ship & iterate ftw). I’m sure we upset some folks, and who knows, maybe we even came off as jerks, but to make big changes sometimes you have to be a little annoying about it. Regardless, it didn’t take long for people internally and externally to see the progress and get on board.

The last big key unlock was taste. I think a lot of the team was stuck in the mindset that this is just how Cloudflare is. Look at AWS, GCP, Azure, this is what big cloud experiences are supposed to be like, confusing and clunky. We came in with the opposite mentality: what are the startups doing? The underdogs, who is building cool developer experiences and how are they doing it? We also brought a lot of Outerbase’s experience into it, again building a dashboard that stays out of your way and lets you ship and manage your data.

Since then it’s been a massive labor of love by a number of extremely talented people. Just about everyone is on the Kumo/redesign bandwagon now and we’re making headway. We purposefully chose not to go the monolith rollout route, again staying true to the mantra and iterating our way through the dashboard page by page.

In practice that’s meant smarter navigation, a Command+K search that finds what you typed, and a DNS records page with a proper table, mobile support, and quick reference built right in. The support portal and member management both got cleaned up, and we shipped generative UI that builds itself when you need it. The feedback so far has been pretty great: “100x better,” “night and day,” and my favorite, “finally feels like a product.”

There are still a number of pages with early Anne Hathaway vibes, but we’re getting there.
