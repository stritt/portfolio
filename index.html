cloudflare’s all that

August 22, 2026

It was about 2 years ago that Brayden and I were sitting in our tiny 120 sqft office heads down doing a major overhaul of Outerbase’s infrastructure, porting it over to Durable Objects and Workers. I remember being so frustrated with the dashboard, but absolutely amazed when it all finally came together.

How could something so powerful be so… difficult? Nothing felt consistent, the pages all loaded slow, and the navigation was incredibly confusing, but the end product was so good it made wading through the muck worth it. We joked that if only they gave us access to their dashboard we could fix her.

Then we got into their startup program, gave some talks together, and ended up being acquired. Since then we’ve been working hard to make that joke a reality, and honestly it hasn’t been as bad as I was thinking, mostly because the problem was never the platform.

Honestly, there was not really any one person or thing to blame either. The dashboard had over 10 years of innovation and releases stacked on top of eachother, shipped by hundreds of developers, each of them building their pages with the best intentions and bringing their own patterns. Every call and decision made sense sense in a silo but there was no glue holding it altogether.

You know that classic 90s movie trope? The one where the “ugly” girl just needed to get a haircut and lose the glasses and all of a sudden she was beautiful? Yeah, that’s Cloudflare. The dashboard needed to get out of its own way and let the actual core primitives shine through.

We were able to make this happen by doing a number of things. First we introduced a design system, what you now may know as Kumo. It started on day 1 with Brayden putting up his first PR to add Tailwind to the dashboard. Surprisingly this was a controversial topic with some developers, and that led to the next key unlock for making the redesign happen.

Being relentless. Thankfully a lot of good people who had a lot of sway had our backs, whether it was us being immovable on design decisions or bringing the startup mentality into building frontend (ship & iterate ftw). I’m sure we upset some folks, and I know I probably came across as a jerk at times, but to make big changes sometimes you have to be a little annoying and unwavering about it. Regardless, it didn’t take long for people internally and externally to see the progress and get on board.

The last big key unlock was agency. I think a lot of the team was stuck in the mindset that this is just how Cloudflare is. Look at AWS, GCP, Azure, this is what big cloud experiences are supposed to be like, confusing and clunky. We came in with the opposite mentality: what are the startups doing? The underdogs, who is building cool developer experiences and how are they doing it? We also brought a lot of Outerbase’s experience into it, again building a dashboard that stays out of your way and lets you ship and manage your data.

Since then it’s been a massive labor of love by a number of extremely talented people. Just about everyone is on the Kumo/redesign bandwagon now and we’re making headway. We purposefully chose not to go the monolith rollout route, again staying true to the mantra and iterating our way through the dashboard page by page.

We started by building out the data studio, and quickly revamped all of storage offerings, then compute, and finally we took on the rest of the dash. Focusing on really nailing the first time user experience and showing how all of the individual pieces can work together. Whether it is showing how resources bind to your worker, protect your website with access, or even speeding up the ability to claim a new domain for your worker, we're working on revamping the entire dash. So far you all seem to be enjoying it: “cloudflare has been getting polished lately,” “feels 10x better than any previous pass,” and my favorite, “every time I log on the UI looks a little nicer.”

There are still a number of pages with early Anne Hathaway vibes, but we’re getting there.
